# 03 — Episode bible: launch's THREE locked stories

**These three are product commitments from the owner's original brief.** No substitution with “The Perfect Apartment,” a conventional ethics quiz or content copied from ANAMNESIS. Each launch story is a 3x3 Ripple Effect: three **initial approaches**, three **follow-up approaches**, an immediate committed consequence, one new fact, nine coherent path summaries and optional social comparisons. The outlines below are **canonical design specifications**, not polished final dialogue. A coding agent may refine copy but must preserve established facts and causal logic.

## Shared editorial contract

- Plausible, contemporary everyday problem; not a test of good/evil.
- First decision must trigger an observable external action or expense. It cannot be undone by choosing differently later.
- Reveal is a **common factual event** across paths, with contextual wording allowed. Do not vary the revelation to punish a particular initial choice.
- Follow-up uses the same three semantic **approach IDs** so the distribution “changed approach” has an honest definition. The wording reflects the first commitment.
- Endings resolve practical outcomes with credible uncertainty. No unearned universal guarantees, fake psychology or deterministic judgments of real-world behavior.
- For each nine-cell route, record: what was done; who knows what; budget/time/trust effects; resolution; an unresolved cost. A route may end positively.
- Writer should ensure no option is absurdly self-destructive merely to make the “right” one obvious. Strong, concise dialogue.
- **No fictional characters should be diagnosed**, and fictional events should not be sold as predictive social science.

# 001 — The €200 Problem

**Theme:** Money · Fairness · Friendship  
**Audience hook:** “Your friend cancels a trip and €200 is missing. Who pays?”  
**Format:** Ripple · ~2 minutes · Warm illustrated reservation/receipt.

### Immutable setup

Four friends — you, Maya, Chris and Leo — have agreed to pay €200 each for a non-refundable €800 cabin. The booking must be settled tonight; you arranged it and remain responsible for paying the operator. Maya cancels two days before the trip and says she cannot contribute. You can temporarily cover the shortfall but it would strain your budget. Everyone else expected to pay €200.

The first choice is an **actual action**, not a private opinion:
- **A / HOLD:** Send Maya a direct request to honor the agreed €200 contribution.
- **B / SHARE:** Tell the group you propose dividing the missing €200 among the three going (~€66.67 extra each, subject to their agreement).
- **C / ABSORB:** Pay the missing €200 yourself now. Your total outgoing payment is €400 including your original share.

### Immediate reaction: not reversible

- A: Maya has received the request. You cannot unsend it.
- B: Chris and Leo have seen the proposal. No additional payment is silently taken from them; their agreement matters.
- C: You have advanced the money. The booking is paid; future reimbursement is possible, but the cash has already left your account.

### Common factual twist

Maya reveals she has unexpectedly lost her job. She can afford **€100**, but not €200 now. Chris privately says *even €34 extra* is difficult for him. Leo has not agreed to cover extra costs. Do not imply Maya is lying or Chris is secretly rich.

### Second decision / the same three approaches, adapted to the committed action

- **A / HOLD:** Seek Maya's remaining €100 via an explicit payment plan, or maintain a request for the full contractual understanding. This is requesting, not magically receiving payment.
- **B / SHARE:** Seek *voluntary* revised allocation: Maya €100, Chris €200, you €250, Leo €250 **if Leo agrees**. If Leo refuses, the organizer must find another plan. On a B-first path acknowledge the group's earlier €66.67 proposal explicitly.
- **C / ABSORB:** Accept Maya's €100 contribution when she can pay and absorb the last €100 yourself. On a C-first path this means a future refund reduces your total expense from €400 to €300. On A/B-first paths the initial message/proposal remains part of the aftermath.

### Nine-route grid: first → final

| First | Final | Minimum causal difference in the authored ending |
|---|---|---|
| A | A | Original direct demand remains; installment arrangement may avoid shared expense but delays recovery and risks tension. You cover outstanding cash temporarily. |
| A | B | You explicitly soften the already-sent demand, acknowledge the new facts, and ask Leo/Chris for voluntary redistribution; Chris cannot afford extra. |
| A | C | You retract the pressure after Maya has read it, advance the remainder yourself, and accept her €100 later. The relationship may heal, but the initial message happened. |
| B | A | Reverse the announced group split, explain the new information while respecting confidentiality, and seek Maya's installment plan. Friends have already seen the first proposal. |
| B | B | Replace the proposed €66.67 extras with a revised *consensual* €50 each for you and Leo; Chris pays only €200 if Leo agrees. Show exact totals. |
| B | C | Withdraw group redistribution, cover the gap yourself, and accept Maya's €100 later; earlier proposal may have unsettled the group. |
| C | A | Booking is already paid by you; you now request Maya's remaining €100 over time, so there is financial exposure but no deadline crisis. |
| C | B | Booking already paid; you ask willing friends for voluntary reimbursements (Maya's €100 plus €50 you and Leo can allocate only with consent; adapt math to avoid double-counting). |
| C | C | You carry €200 initially; Maya pays you €100 later, leaving you with €300 total instead of €200. Chris/Leo retain their original shares. |

**Math invariant:** the €800 operator invoice is paid by actual amounts, not by a suggestion. If Maya eventually pays €100 and Chris €200, the remaining €500 is paid by you and Leo. In revised-share B, you €250 and Leo €250 → €800 total. For any initial advance, treat subsequent transfers as reimbursements, not a second payment to the operator. If Leo has not agreed, describe the plan as pending and let the player cover the temporary gap; never narrate consent that was not earned.

**Behind the decision (optional):** sunk costs versus future costs, fairness as agreement vs ability to pay, consent, early commitments and updating with new information. Don't present a single correct ethics framework.

**Art beats:** reservation slip (€800) → one charge/message → new note from Maya and Chris → balanced ledger / outcome. The exact allocation must reconcile.

# 002 — The Unfair Promotion

**Theme:** Merit · Loyalty · Incomplete information  
**Audience hook:** “Two employees. One gets the results. The other makes the results possible. Who gets promoted?”  
**Format:** Ripple · 2 minutes · Warm office-board and contribution cards.

### Distinction from existing ANAMNESIS story

ANAMNESIS has a room named “promotion” about a *friend's costly mistake and reporting it to win the job*. THIS IS NOT THAT STORY. THE VERDICT episode is about making an organizational recommendation with incomplete evidence and uncertain role criteria. No secret betrayal, sabotage or getting a promotion for yourself.

### Immutable setup

You lead a small team. A new **team lead** position opens. You must submit a recommendation to your manager today.

- **Iris:** visibly strong individual delivery; successful client presentation; clear written work and deadlines.
- **Noah:** reliable teammate who supports colleagues, fixes recurring issues and often enables others' success; less visible in formal dashboards.
- You have insufficient data on their leadership experience or preferences. The new role involves **both** coaching the team and communicating with clients.

### First decision / actual action

- **A / IRIS:** Submit a recommendation for Iris, emphasizing delivery and client credibility.
- **B / NOAH:** Submit a recommendation for Noah, emphasizing team enablement and reliability.
- **C / REVIEW:** Ask for a short evidence-based review before naming either candidate. This may be viewed as prudent or slow; you have acted by requesting a delay.

### Immediate reaction

- A/B: manager has seen your preliminary recommendation and may start planning around it.
- C: manager has received a request for more evidence and warns the position is time-sensitive. You have not made a final nomination.

### Common twist

A post-project review surfaces previously untracked work: Noah quietly prevented a critical release failure and trained two teammates whose output appears in the metrics. Iris, however, handled a difficult client negotiation that protected a renewal. Neither committed misconduct; both contributions matter. Crucially, the hiring manager clarifies that *the new role requires coaching and client-facing leadership*, but does not reveal which candidate will perform better.

### Second decision / comparable final stance

- **A / IRIS:** Maintain or revise toward Iris, with a transparent account of Noah's contribution and a plan to address management skills.
- **B / NOAH:** Maintain or revise toward Noah, with a transparent account of Iris's performance and a plan for client-facing development.
- **C / REVIEW:** Recommend a structured, time-boxed assessment of leadership readiness, with a concrete date and criteria. No magical third job or automatic co-lead role.

### Nine-route grid

| First | Final | Authored path consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | A | You keep Iris after documenting Noah's invisible work. Fast recommendation, some remaining coaching uncertainty. |
| A | B | You send a revision of your submitted Iris recommendation and explain why evidence changed it; speed/credibility trade-off. |
| A | C | You request a hold after initially naming Iris; explain the evidence gap, and respect the manager's timetable. |
| B | A | You reverse an initial Noah recommendation in light of client-facing requirements; Noah's contribution must still be recognized. |
| B | B | You reaffirm Noah with evidence of coaching; client development remains an identifiable risk. |
| B | C | You step back from Noah despite early endorsement; potential lost momentum in exchange for a more defensible evaluation. |
| C | A | Your requested review collects useful data and leads to Iris; hiring is delayed slightly but criteria are explicit. |
| C | B | The review surfaces Noah's case and leads to Noah; timeline pressure remains. |
| C | C | You continue with a narrowly scoped assessment; this preserves neutrality but may lose the available promotion window. |

The story must avoid implying either candidate is objectively the correct hire without more data. Give a meaningful ending that acknowledges team effects. The strongest reveal is about *what metrics conceal*, not a morality lecture.

**Behind the decision:** availability bias, Goodhart-like metric fixation, credit attribution, time pressure, deciding under uncertainty. Avoid citing disputed research as established fact.

**Art beats:** recommendation card sent → two activity histories → “new evidence” overlays → revised memo with final approach.

# 003 — The AI Shortcut

**Theme:** Trust · Technology · Responsibility  
**Audience hook:** “AI finished your report in ten minutes. Would you send it?”  
**Format:** Ripple · ~2 minutes · Warm desk/document interface.

### Immutable setup

A client-facing project update is due today. You have reliable raw notes and an AI-generated draft that looks unusually polished, including a chart and a summary of recent results. You remain accountable for the final document. You have enough time to do **some** checking, but comprehensive verification may miss the planned delivery time.

### First decision / actual action

- **A / SHIP:** Send the draft immediately with no substantial verification. Recipient receives it.
- **B / VERIFY:** Hold delivery, spot-check the most important figures and citations first. Verification consumes time and may still miss issues.
- **C / DISCLOSE:** Tell the recipient the draft used AI and agree on a verification plan before delivery. Transparency uses time but may improve expectations.

### Immediate reaction

- A: the client now possesses the draft.
- B: the draft is held; the deadline gets closer, and you gather some evidence.
- C: the client has been told about AI assistance and verification status, not merely a vague promise.

### Common twist

A colleague notices the chart mixes two different reporting periods, making the headline comparison misleading **even though the underlying numbers are real**. The client now asks for the underlying source and wants the report for a meeting. The same defect existed on every path: no magical AI malice, no fabricated data, no claim that all AI output is unreliable.

### Second decision / comparable final stance

- **A / SHIP:** Move ahead with a rapid correction/addendum or release the remaining material with limitations; do not say a previously sent report was never sent.
- **B / VERIFY:** Correct the chart, attach reproducible data and deliver the revised version, possibly late.
- **C / DISCLOSE:** Transparently explain the issue, indicate what is verified and negotiate a short revised deadline or scoped release.

### Nine-route grid

| First | Final | Minimum distinct consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | A | Draft was already sent; rapid addendum limits damage but client may have reused the original chart. |
| A | B | You send a correction and verified source later, with a clear version history; trust can recover but not erase exposure. |
| A | C | You contact client proactively, disclose the issue/process and arrange replacement; accountability is visible after a risky release. |
| B | A | You did some verification before a hurried release; the earlier check influences what you know but did not resolve every issue. |
| B | B | You finish checking and deliver corrected sources; may miss the planned deadline with stronger data. |
| B | C | You disclose uncertainties and agree on a scoped deliverable; initial verification helps define exactly what is safe. |
| C | A | Client was informed earlier; rapid release is within a disclosed provisional scope, but a correction may still be required. |
| C | B | Expectations were set; you deliver a revised, verified report with a manageable delay. |
| C | C | Explicitly renegotiate scope with transparent status; client can plan around what is known, but work is not instantly finished. |

**Tone:** pro-competence, not anti-AI. The challenge is responsibility and managing uncertainty. It must not imply disclosure alone corrects inaccuracies or checking eliminates every risk.

**Behind the decision:** calibrated trust, verification effort, automation bias, communication, time–quality trade-offs. Science claims must be source-checked.

**Art beats:** ready-to-send document → sent/held/annotated state → the chart's conflicting period highlighted → version history / source attachment / outcome.

## 4. Content acceptance test for all three

For each scenario:
1. Read all 9 paths aloud and ask whether the first action still matters.
2. Check every number, date and causal statement against the invariant facts.
3. Confirm all three first choices are credible; all three second approaches remain feasible.
4. Verify that the “new information” does not change across branches to manipulate outcomes.
5. Check each route differs in at least one concrete external consequence, not just adjective.
6. Verify tone: approachable, thoughtful, occasionally warm, never scolding.
7. Verify authorship and spoiler-free sharing.
8. Confirm no hidden claim about legal duties, employment policy, financial advice or medical/psychological effects.
9. Add unit tests for all nine routes per episode and test at least three routes visually.
10. Get an owner editorial pass before declaring the episode finished.
